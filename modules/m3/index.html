Teach — Three patterns that actually scale

Practice will ask you to pick one of the three taught patterns: shared-doc + peer poll (Peer Instruction), fishbowl, or interrupted/staged case. For each: what it is, why it scales, what you do as instructor, when to choose it.

A. Shared-doc breakouts + summarizer + Peer Instruction

Teams work in a visible shared doc. One person (the summarizer) posts a short written summary. Then the whole room answers a poll on a process fork—researchers call this Peer Instruction (PI): individual vote → short peer talk if the room is mixed (~30–70% correct) → re-vote; you cold-queue a few roster numbers (call on specific people by list number, not volunteers only). Why it scales: everyone contributes in writing; synthesis is whole-cohort; you never promised every-group airtime.

B. Online fishbowl

A small inner panel runs a timed segment while everyone else watches with a job. Outer cohort completes an observation checklist (a tool for participants—not a module “tick every box” list). That’s a fishbowl: few people perform; many people observe with structure. Why it scales: depth for a few; structured looking for the rest; rotate panels across sessions so more people get inner-ring time over time.

C. Interrupted / staged case

Reveal the case in stages. Individuals commit short answers before group talk. You synthesize themes. Why it scales: individual accountability before talk; staged reveals keep pacing without visiting every room.

Subgroup size bands & what still fails

Useful size bands (rough guides): ~8–10 for discussion; ~4–6 for process teams; ~20–30 for larger discussion sets. Pair sizes with roles, protocols, exemplars, and whole-room theme notes. Still fails: every-group oral report; you visit all rooms; “same breakouts but fewer groups” if oral report is still how you collect evidence.

Worked example — Shared-doc + Peer Instruction redesign

Non-scalable segment: 15-min breakouts (~30 groups) + random oral sample of 3.

Strong — shared-doc + PI
  • ~4–6 per process team; named summarizer
  • Visible shared doc + 60-sec written summary
  • Peer Instruction: poll → short discuss if the room is mixed → re-poll; cold-call 4 people by roster number
  • Instructor watches for flags only; shares 3 whole-room theme notes (broadcast synthesis)
Weak

“Same breakouts but only 10 groups” — still fails if oral report remains the evidence path.

Here’s a strong pattern pick

Fishbowl or an interrupted/staged case are fine alternate picks from the three taught patterns. If you only name a gallery item—role-split/reassemble, choose-your-path, and so on—without one of those three (shared-doc + peer poll / fishbowl / interrupted case), go back and pick from the three taught patterns.

What “good enough” looks like

  • Non-scalable pattern named · Replacement is one of the three taught patterns: shared-doc + peer poll (PI), fishbowl, interrupted/staged case
  • Subgroup size / roles · Timing + purpose · Instructor bandwidth realistic with zero TA help
  • Directions present · No every-group airtime or visit-every-room
