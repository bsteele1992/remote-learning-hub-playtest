Quick prompt (not scored): What evidence do you actually need—and what can one instructor honestly review?

Teach — Mid-session checks that survive 1:124

A. Decision frame: minutes-per-learner first

Formative assessment means evidence you use to adjust teaching mid-flight—not a tiny final exam. Before you pick a method, budget how many minutes one instructor can spend per learner (or per sample). That’s your minutes-per-learner limit again.

B. Methods that work (what evidence + why it scales)

  • Peer Instruction / response polls at process decision points — auto-tally; short discuss window when the room is mixed; you explain the pattern.
  • Interrupted case — individual short answers before group dialogue.
  • Structured worksheets / process templates — visible rows you can sample.
  • Protocol peer feedback on drafts — peers carry the load; you arbitrate outliers.
  • Auto-graded quizzes with elaborated feedback — good for prerequisites; not the only check that people can apply the process.

C. Whole-room themes vs reply-all

Prefer whole-cohort theme notes from a sample over private replies to every submission. That’s broadcast synthesis again. Reply-all presence collapses under volume.

Worked example — Weak vs strong formative

Strong

Peer Instruction on a bike-teaching decision fork: everyone votes alone, then if the room is mixed (~30–70% correct) they discuss and re-vote. Next, sample 8 of 124 template rows in 10 minutes and share 3 whole-room theme notes. Minutes-per-learner are budgeted—this is not 124 private replies.

Weak

“Review every chat message” / “grade all breakout notes tonight” — scale executability fails. One person cannot do that honestly.

Strong Peer Instruction sketch

Poll prompt: “Training wheels are still on, but the learner wants to try without them. Best next move?” Give A/B/C/D choices (keep options obvious—e.g. keep wheels / try three meters with a spotter). Run the Peer Instruction loop (solo vote → short discuss if mixed → re-vote), then give your 90-second explanation.

What “good enough” looks like

  • Evidence tied to application objective · Method from the grounded formative menu
  • Executable at 100+ with zero TA help · Sampling / auto / peer-protocol / timebox stated
  • Minutes-per-learner or equivalent budget noted · Not awareness-only quiz as the only check
